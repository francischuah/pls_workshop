Theoretical and forged concepts

Your conceptualisation determines what the model means.

01

Theoretical concept

A concept whose existence is theorised independently of how it is measured. Example: an attitude.

02

Forged concept

An artefact defined through a combination of components. Example: a deliberately constructed index.

MEASUREMENT / BASIC PLS-SEM · 23

Why square a loading?

Indicator reliability is about variance accounted for.

.80² = .64

An illustrative loading of .80 corresponds to 64% explained indicator variance.

A strong loading is useful evidence, but it does not establish all aspects of measurement quality.
MEASUREMENT / BASIC PLS-SEM · 24

Internal consistency

Do the reflective indicators work together reliably?

StatisticWhat it contributesTypical reading
Cronbach’s αTraditional consistency estimateInterpret alongside the other reliability estimates.

ρC / CRComposite reliabilityCommon working range .70–.95.
Very high reliability can suggest redundant items. Reliability is not validity.
MEASUREMENT / BASIC PLS-SEM · 25

Average variance extracted

Do the indicators capture enough of their construct?

AVE = mean(λ²)

Illustration: (.80² + .75² + .85²) ÷ 3 = .642

AVE ≥ .50 indicates that the construct accounts for at least half of its indicators’ variance on average.
MEASUREMENT / BASIC PLS-SEM · 26

HTMT: are constructs distinct?

Discriminant validity concerns pairs of reflective constructs.

01

Point estimate

Use a justified .85 or .90 threshold.

02

Inference

Examine whether the bootstrap upper bound is below the chosen critical threshold.

03

Problem detected

Inspect conceptual overlap, item wording and model specification.

A CI that only excludes 1 does not establish that HTMT is below .85 or .90.

STRUCTURAL / BASIC PLS-SEM · 39

R²: explained variance

How much variance in the endogenous construct is accounted for?

55% explained
45% remaining

Illustration: R² = .55 for CUSL.

Interpret relative to the field, outcome and model. R² is not evidence of out-of-sample predictive performance.
STRUCTURAL / BASIC PLS-SEM · 40

f²: contribution to explained variance

How much does explained variance change when one predictor is removed?

f² = (R²included − R²excluded) / (1 − R²included)

Illustration: (.55 − .50) / (1 − .55) = .111

.02 Small.15 Medium.35 Large
STRUCTURAL / BASIC PLS-SEM · 41

Report the finding, then its meaning

Keep significance, effect size and business interpretation connected.

Illustration: COMP was positively associated with CUSL (β = .25, 95% CI [.10, .40]). Its incremental contribution was small by the conventional f² reference points (f² = .111). The model explained 55% of CUSL variance (R² = .55).
This supports an association under the specified model; a causal claim requires additional research-design justification.

PREDICTION / BASIC PLS-SEM · 45

What are IA and LM?

Two benchmarks: a mean-based guess and a regression prediction.

01 · IA

Indicator average

Use each target indicator’s training-sample mean to predict every held-out case. No predictor information is used.

Question: Can our model beat simply guessing the mean?

02 · LM

Linear model

Fit an ordinary linear regression for each target indicator, using the exogenous indicators as predictors. Estimate it on training cases and predict held-out cases.

Question: Does our specified PLS path model predict better than regression without its construct/path structure?

PLSpredict uses both benchmarks: Q²predict > 0 means lower squared error than IA. Compare RMSE or MAE with LM separately; beating IA does not guarantee beating LM.
PREDICTION / BASIC PLS-SEM · 46

RMSE and MAE

Prediction error: lower values are better for the same target.

01

RMSE

Root mean squared error: gives more weight to large errors.

02

MAE

Mean absolute error: average absolute prediction error.

03

Benchmark

Compare the same indicator, error metric and holdout procedure.

Choose the error metric consistently; do not select whichever gives a more favourable conclusion.
